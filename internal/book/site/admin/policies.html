Maestro

Choosing the policies

An administrator sets the scheduler up on the Maestro page, under Admin › Settings. Policies and the score explains what each policy and weight does.

A saved change applies from the scheduler's next pass, to lines that haven't moved yet. Lines already assigned stay where they are.

Starting from a preset

The Scheduler section has four buttons under "Start from". Each one fills in the fields listed for it and leaves the others alone. Nothing is saved until you press "Save changes", and Discard puts back what was saved last.

PresetWhat it setsThe queue it gives
FIFO (baseline)All three policies to fifo, all four weights to 0Lines go in the order they were queued. This is how a new Maestro starts.
DRFdispatcher = pq, share = drf, ω4 = −1, the other weights 0The user who holds the least of the machines now goes next.
Fair-sharedispatcher = pq, share = decay, ω4 = −1, the other weights 0The user who used the least recently goes next.
Fair, with agingdispatcher = pq, share = drf, ω3 = 1, ω4 = −1, the other weights 0As DRF, and a line gains on the others the longer it waits.

The sections of the page

Scheduler

This section holds the policy of each of the three queues.

LabelSettingCan be
User queueuserfifo, lcfs, siro, pq
Dispatcher queuedispatcherfifo, lcfs, siro, pq
Machine queuemachineThose four, and fifo-skip, best-fit, weighted-best-fit, tetris

It also holds "Waiting on a machine" (depth, 3 by default) and the seed that makes siro and the draw of a machine repeat.

Fairness

This section holds the share measure, the four weights of the score, the waiting horizon in minutes and the half-life in hours.

It also holds the user weights that weighted-drf divides by. Add a row for each user who should count differently from the rest, with the user's number and a weight for cores and for memory. Everyone without a row has 1 for both. maestro queue shows an administrator each user's number, as in Ada #7.

Limits

LabelSettingDefault
Images per userimages_per_user10, and at most 1000
Cores per useruser_max_cpus0, which means no ceiling
Days a run is keptrun_retention_days0, which means until someone deletes it, and at most 3650

Lowering "Images per user" removes nobody's images at that moment. A user who is over the new number loses images at their next build, which removes as many of the ones they ran least recently as it takes to make room. The build is refused only when runs are still using all the images that are left.

"Cores per user" is a ceiling on the cores each user holds. A user's lines assigned, starting and running count toward it, and their queued lines don't. In a group's turn, a user's head isn't a candidate while its cores, added to those, would be over the ceiling. The heads of other users go as usual. Lowering it stops no line. It only holds back a user's next lines until enough of theirs end. A line that asks for more cores than the ceiling never starts, and while it is its user's head, their lines behind it wait too. maestro queue tells a user when their next line waits for the ceiling.

"Days a run is kept" has orq delete each run, with its files, that many days after its last line ended. A run with a line still waiting or running doesn't expire, and neither does one its owner kept with maestro runs keep. Lowering it deletes the runs it makes old within the hour. What orq keeps says how.

Checking what is in force

maestro settings prints the saved settings for any user, and maestro queue ends with a one-line summary of them.

Each line records the settings it was assigned under and the group its user was in. An export that spans a change of settings therefore still says which settings served which line.

Settings saved before orq had three queues had a select policy, which chose among a group's users. orq reads it as the dispatcher policy, so a pq there stays pq. Their allocate and staged_capacity no longer set anything.

If orq can't read the saved settings after an upgrade, it falls back to FIFO in every queue so that lines keep starting, and its log says why. Save the settings again to fix it.

What administrators changed

orq records every change an administrator makes to the settings, to the groups and who is in them, and to the machines. It also records each time an administrator revokes a user's Maestro keys. A record says who made the change, when, what it was, and the values before and after it, as JSON. Admin › History lists the records, newest first, 50 at a time. Show what changed lists the fields that differ.

A record never holds a secret. A machine's record has its address, its host key, its reserve and the names of its proxy variables, but not its SSH key or the values of those variables. Revoked keys are named by their prefixes.

Saving the settings without changing them records nothing. A refused change records nothing, and saves none of what it asked for.

The export of lines doesn't include the records. When you measure a period for the thesis, History shows when the settings changed, so you can cut the period there. Each line also keeps the settings it was assigned under.

The records are in orq's database, and orq keeps them as long as the database. GET /api/admin/actions returns them, and with before set to a record's id, the 50 older than it.

Trying policies before choosing

Comparing policies offline runs the same scheduler over a recorded or generated workload, so you can compare two setups without waiting for the lab to live through them.
